©Ingo Arnold 8■Enterprise, Domain, Solution, and Software Architecture
•Enterprise Architecture (EA) and Domain Architecture (DA) consider the entire system 
landscape of an organization or domain as their design object .
•Enterprise Architecture : focuses on alignment between business strategy , processes, and IT capabilities .
•Domain Architecture : focuses on specific business/technical domains and the architecture of the domain as a whole .
•Both operate at a strategic and cross- system level .
•Solution Architecture (SA) focuses on the architecture of a single solution (system ).
•Ensures that the solution meets all functional and non-functional requirements , constraints , and assumptions . 
•Balances business needs, technical feasibility , and integration constraints .
•Operates ata project or solution delivery level .
•Software Architecture (SWA) is a subset of Solution Architecture concerned with the internal
structure of the software being designed and implemented .
•Defines how software components are structured and interact to fulfill required functionality and quality attributes .Wrap -Up

•Functional or operational components already available (e.g., existing services, databases, middleware).
•Examples: integration APIs , message queues, authentication systems .
•Parts that need to be designed
•New components developed specifically for the current solution .
•The software architect focuses on this part – designing the internal architecture while ensuring proper integration with existing 
systems.Wrap -Up

©Ingo Arnold 10■Software Architect ’s core Responsibilities
•Use Case / Scenario Realizations
•Designing components and their collaborations to fulfill functional requirements .
•Focus on system behavior and control flow for actor use cases or scenarios.
•Quality Attribute Scenario (QAR) Realizations
•Ensuring non-functional requirements (e.g., performance, scalability, security, modifiability).
•Includes architectural mechanisms such as caching , redundancy , monitoring , or abstraction .
•Business Components (Domain Model)
•Defining and structuring the core domain logic and related business components .
•Represents key entities, services, and rules that embody business knowledge .
•All three fields above must be balanced and interconnected to create a coherent software 
architecture.Wrap -Up
