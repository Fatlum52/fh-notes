Lecture Opening
Motivation
Microservices are not a trend , but a response to real organizational and 
technical scaling problems .
Traditional monoliths provide centralized simplicity , but suffer from (a) tight
coupling of UI/logic/data, (b) limited modifiability and deployment inflexibility , as 
well as (c) slow scaling.
Microservices enable independent deployment , team ownership , 
autonomous pipelines , fine-grained scalability , improved modularization & 
decoupling, and reuse across applications, even unknown future contexts.
The principles remain the same (modularity, abstraction, SoC, explicit 
interfaces), but microservices push them further .

Motivation
Microservices are not a passing trend
10The fundamental principles for high-quality architectures have remained
largely unchanged –both for monolithic and for microservice-based applications .
Modularity & Abstraction . Clear Building Blocks with explicit interfaces promote loose coupling and 
high cohesion. 
Information Hiding & Separation of Concerns . Hide internal details and clearly separate 
responsibilities as a basis for interchangeability.
Design for Change & Open/Closed . Design with change in mind –keep Building Blocks open for 
extension as well as closed against uncontrolled access.
Explicit Interfaces & Interface Segregation . Design stable contracts with low commitment to 
respective implementations.
Traceability & Self- Documentation . Decisions and structures are traceable, and anchored in each 
respective artifact.
Basically all Architecture Principles aim 
at reducing complexity and gaining 
flexibility –especially through loose 
coupling and high cohesion.

Definition
Overview
19How Microservice Architectures are defined in the Software Industry …
The distinctive characteristics of Microservice 
Architectures and hence their differentiation from 
monolithic Architectures can be highlighted
particularly well using the 4+1 View Model .A Microservice Architecture organizes an application into a collection of loosely coupled , 
fine-grained services that communicate via lightweight protocols . 
A Microservice Architecture is characterized by the ability to develop and deploy services
independently , improving modularity, scalability, and adaptability. 
However, it introduces additional complexity , particularly in managing distributed systems
and inter- service communication , making the initial implementation more challenging 
compared to a monolithic architecture . [Wikipedia]

Pattern Language –Messaging Pattern
54The Messaging Pattern replaces synchronous service dependencies with 
asynchronous message exchange. 
The Messaging Pattern is a 
cornerstone of scalable, resilient , 
and decoupled architectures …
… However , it requires careful 
design of message semantics , 
delivery guarantees , and monitoring
to maintain system integrity .
The Messaging Pattern connects
services via a Message Bus (or Pub- Sub 
Topic ) that allows them to transfer data 
messages asynchronously . 
Services that form the distributed 
system connect with the message 
bus to exchange data messages 
with other services. 
Clients can initiate
collaborations with remote 
services by sending them data 
messages asynchronously . 
The remote services process the received
messages and return their responses (if 
there are any), asynchronously to the 
clients via messages containing the 
processing results .

56Two types of parsers exist for parsing XML input . 
Both types differ in their approach to parsing, with each parser type having its 
strengths and weaknesses.
DOM (i.e., document object model) Parsers …
allow programs to access and update the content and structure of XML documents dynamically .
read an XML document into memory and represent it as a tree; each node of the tree represents a 
particular piece of data from the original XML document.
because the tree structure is a standard programming mechanism for representing data, 
traversing and manipulating the tree using Java is relatively easy , fast, and efficient .
main drawback is that the entire XML document has to be read into memory to create the tree. 
This might decrease the performance of an application as the XML documents get larger .

Organizational Replication
110Melvin Conway researched and described the phenomenon of organizational 
replication (i.e., the impact organizational forces have on Systems) already in 1968.
Organizational replication is a 
phenomenon you can observe
at all levels of architecture . 
At the enterprise level , you can 
anticipate the architecture of enterprise
systems by examining the design of the 
enterprise operating model . 
At the domain level , you examine the 
structures of your domain organizations 
in order to anticipate the architecture of 
your Big Systems (aka: domains). 
At the solution level , you explore the 
structures of projects or operationally
responsible organizations to predict your 
landscape asset designs (e.g., the design
of a new application ). 
The enterprise’s organizational structure is 
replicated in your enterprise system architecture .

Organizational Replication and Microservices
111In the context the organizational replication phenomenon, a few aspects are to 
specifically be considered , regarding microservices-oriented solution architecture .
Technical organizations tend to orient 
themselves towards technical 
structures in software design (e.g., 
technically oriented layer architecture ).
In the context of 
microservices, 
this approach 
must be avoided.
Rather technical organizations must adapt the 
organizational structures of the business organization to 
design microservices with a focus on business capabilities .

Organizational Replication and Microservices
113In the context the organizational replication phenomenon, a few aspects are to 
specifically be considered , regarding microservices-oriented solution architecture .
The advantage of an overarching
application that is modularized only 
internally is that it offers its modules
a comprehensive context .
Contextual aspects that are 
automatically shared by all 
modules include , e.g., value 
systems, privilege models , or 
domain ontologies .
The lack of an application- wide context in 
microservices means that services must be 
contextualized much more explicitly in terms of domain 
ontologies , privilege models , value systems, etc., or 
that the necessary contextual information must be 
organized by the services themselves.

 115■Definition and Core Characteristics
•Microservice architectures consist of loosely coupled , fine-grained services that can be developed , 
tested , deployed , and scaled independently .
•Communication is vialightweight protocols (REST, events, messaging).
•Advantages are …
•Higher modularity
•Scalability
•Team autonomy
•Disadvantages are …
•Increased distributed system complexity
•Observability challenges
•Operational overheadWrap -Up

 116■Architecture Patterns
•Communication Patterns …
•API Gateway . Single entry point, routing, aggregation, security, protocol translation.
•Service Registry . Manages dynamic service endpoints for discovery.
•Messaging . Asynchronous decoupling, MOM guarantees, XML- based messaging
•Layered System in Microservices. Domain- oriented vertical slices instead of horizontal mega- layers.
•Scalability Patterns (X/Y/Z). X: cloning operational nodes, Y: functional decomposition, Z: partitioning by data- segment.
•Hexagonal Architecture
•Business logic is isolated by (a) inbound ports/adapters (controllers, message subscribers) and (b) outbound ports/adapters (DB 
adapters, service proxies).
•Perfectly aligns with microservice design.Wrap -Up

 117■Persistence Patterns
•Database per Service …
•Each Microservice owns its private schema and database.
•No shared Database access.
•Enables autonomy but requires new consistency mechanisms.
•Cross -Service Consistency Mechanisms
•Event Sourcing Pattern stores changes as events, not state. On the other side downstream services reconstruct local state.
•CDC (Change Data Capture) extracts Database changes from transaction logs and publishes them as events.
•Saga Pattern organizes distributed transactions via local ACID steps as well as compensation actions.
■Security Patterns
•Firewall Proxy …
•Filters requests before reaching microservices.
•Declarative rule base for payload inspection and access control.
•Stateful versions skip repeated checks for established sessions.
•RBAC
•Access rights assigned to roles, not individuals. Further, RBAC supports role hierarchies.
•XACML Model
•Privilege system with PAP, PIP, PDP, PEP supports attribute -based access control (ABAC) for complex policies.Wrap -Up

 118■Organizational Impact
•Conway's Law …
•Organizations replicate their communication structures in system design.
•Microservices require business -aligned, not technically aligned, team structures.
•Product Teams (“You build it, you run it”)
•Each microservice team is responsible for the full lifecycle.
•Opposes project -driven delivery structures.
•Loss of Application -Wide Context
•Without a monolith’s global context, microservices must explicitly maintain (a) domain ontologies, (b) privilege models as well as 
(c) value systems.
•Cultural Preconditions
•Success requires willingness to share and reuse services across the organization.Wrap -Up
