Overview
4In this exercise, you will be given requirements for a new system that you are 
expected to design and develop. 
For each requirement , you need to consider how architecture-significant it is.
A requirement is significant if the software design to which the requirement is 
likely to lead is a design that is difficult to dismantle , change or adapt should the 
requirement change in the future .

15Requirement 1: The system must be highly available 24/7.
Architecture Significance: Yes.Resulting Architecture Decisions: 
This requirement usually leads to the introduction of redundancy (clusters, load balancers, 
failover systems), distributed system components and, if necessary, separate monitoring 
infrastructure.
Why difficult to change Architecture Decisions later : 
Once implemented, a high- availability setup affects deployment, testability, operating models 
and monitoring. 
It is expensive to change if, for example, requirements change towards 5x9 availability 
(instead of 24/7) or lower operating requirements.example

can be used to provide a substitute or

object , allowing something to be executed

An important goal here is to structure
Microservices in such a way that they 
are lightweight , independently
deployable , and scalable.
Another software 
architecture principle is called 
service statelessness.
This principle requires that a 
service does not store any 
information between service-
requests, orthat it fully delegates 
persisting any status information 
to specific components.
This is illustrated by the many 
equivalent instances of the 
order service.
Each request (e.g., to 
the order service) is 
hence treated as an 
independent, complete 
transaction .
This offers major 
advantages such as …
… easy scalability (since any 
server can process the request )
high fault tolerance (since the failure of one 
server does not affect the overall status).
… and improved agility and maintainability , since the 
complexity of status management is delegated to 
external components (e.g., central database).

 83■Objective of the Exercise
•The goal is to identify which requirements are architecturally significant and to reason about
why they are .
•Architecturally significant requirements (ASRs) are those that:
•Influence fundamental design decisions.
•Lead to structures that are difficult to change once implemented.
•Affect multiple components , layers , or quality attributes of the system.
•Students analyse requirements , identify ASRs , and design coarse -grained components as well 
as more detailed architectural approaches for selected set of requirements .Wrap -Up

 84■Understanding Significance
•A requirement is architecture- significant if it strongly shapes the system’s structure or 
behaviour .
•Indicators of significance :
•Impacts non- functional qualities (e.g., availability, performance, scalability, security).
•Requires new architectural mechanisms or cross -cutting solutions.
•Drives irreversible design choices (platforms, frameworks, integration approaches).
•Conversely , non-significant requirements typically affect only local elements (UI, formatting, 
small validation rules).Wrap -Up

 87■Misleadingly “Non-Significant” Requirements
•Some requirements may seem trivial at first but later have deep architectural consequences :
•Modifiable UI : configurable UI frameworks, meta- layer for field definitions.

•Roles and rights: may evolve into attribute- based access control (ABAC).
•Export /reporting: may lead to data warehouse, ETL pipelines, and OLAP systems.
